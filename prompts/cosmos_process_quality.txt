## Analytical Focus

You must evaluate the trace against these industrial quality criteria:

1. **Process capability (Cpk mindset)** — For each screwing cycle, measure the key process parameters: CW stroke angular range, stroke duration, lift height consistency, CCW reposition range, and re-engage position accuracy. A production-ready process should show < 5% variation across cycles. Flag any parameter with cycle-to-cycle variation > 10%.

2. **Takt time analysis** — Break the total execution time into productive time (CW screwing while engaged) vs. non-productive time (transit, lift, CCW reposition, settling, idle). Calculate the productive-to-total ratio. For a competitive screwing station, productive time should be > 40% of total cycle time. Below 30% indicates the process needs redesign.

3. **First-pass yield indicators** — Scan for any event that would cause a reject on a real production line: gripper slip (gripper value change during screwing), cross-threading indicators (abnormal resistance pattern in wrist_3 velocity), incomplete engagement (CW rotation starting before arm is fully lowered), or missed cycles.

4. **Positional repeatability** — Compare the arm joint positions (j1–j5) at each screw engagement moment across all cycles. For production quality, engagement position should repeat within ±0.005 rad. Variation beyond ±0.01 rad suggests the IK solutions are not converging to consistent configurations, which would cause alignment issues with real screw heads.

5. **Cycle time predictability** — For each of the N cycles, measure the total cycle time (engage → CW stroke → lift → CCW → re-engage). A predictable process should have < 8% coefficient of variation across cycles. High variation suggests the motion planner is finding different paths each time.

6. **Torque proxy analysis** — Without direct torque data, use wrist_3 velocity profile during CW strokes as a proxy. Constant velocity suggests free spinning (screw not engaged or stripped). Gradually decreasing velocity suggests increasing resistance (proper screw advancement). Sudden velocity drops suggest jamming or thread contact.

7. **End-of-line readiness** — Does the robot leave the workspace in a known, clean state? Is the screwdriver properly handled (placed back or held securely)? Are all joints returned to a home position that allows the next part to be loaded? Any residual joint offset > 0.01 rad from home is a line-stop condition.

## Evaluation Rubric

Score each dimension from 1 (worst) to 5 (best). Provide a brief justification for each.

### A. Reasoning Quality

1. **Spatial Awareness** (1-5): Does the reasoning correctly identify the screwdriver position, the screw target location, the required top-down grasp orientation, and the geometric relationship between grasp height, approach height, and engage height?

2. **Phase Identification** (1-5): Does the reasoning correctly segment the trajectory into the expected phases — pick (home, open, approach, descend, close, retreat), screw (move above target, lower to engage, CW rotation, lift, CCW reposition, re-engage per cycle), and return (lift off, home)? Are phases correctly bounded by timestep ranges?

3. **Screwing Mechanics Understanding** (1-5): Does the reasoning demonstrate understanding of the cyclic screwing strategy — that CW rotation drives the screw only while engaged, that CCW reposition must happen while lifted to avoid unscrewing, and that wrist_3 has a finite range requiring the lift-reposition-reengage cycle?

4. **Safety Awareness** (1-5): Does the reasoning account for wrist_3 joint limits (staying within the safe margin), gripper stability during screwing (no unexpected gripper state changes), collision avoidance during transitions between screwdriver pickup and screw target, and proper sequencing (never CCW while engaged)?

### B. Trajectory Quality — Pick Phase

5. **Grasp Execution** (1-5): Based on the trajectory, did the robot correctly approach the screwdriver from above, descend to the proper grasp height, close the gripper to the expected opening value for the screwdriver diameter, and retreat cleanly upward without lateral drift?
   - 5 = top-down approach, correct height, correct gripper value, clean vertical retreat
   - 3 = minor misalignment or slightly off gripper value but likely functional
   - 1 = clearly wrong approach angle, wrong height, or gripper did not close appropriately

6. **Pick-to-Screw Transition** (1-5): Did the robot transition smoothly from the retreat position above the screwdriver to the approach position above the screw target? Was the tool orientation maintained as top-down throughout?
   - 5 = smooth lateral repositioning with consistent orientation
   - 3 = some unnecessary waypoints or minor orientation deviation
   - 1 = large detour, orientation flip, or trajectory suggesting potential collision

### C. Trajectory Quality — Screwing Phase

7. **Screw Engagement** (1-5): Did the robot lower to the correct engage height at the screw target XY position before beginning wrist rotation? Was the top-down orientation preserved?
   - 5 = precise XY alignment and correct Z, orientation maintained
   - 3 = slight positional offset but likely still on the screw head
   - 1 = clearly misaligned or wrong height

8. **CW Stroke Quality** (1-5): During CW wrist rotation steps, were joints 1-5 held approximately constant (pure wrist rotation)? Were the step sizes consistent and in the correct direction (wrist_3 decreasing)? Did wrist_3 stay within the safe range?
   - 5 = only wrist_3 changes, consistent steps, correct direction, within limits
   - 3 = minor drift in other joints, mostly correct stepping
   - 1 = significant arm movement during screwing, wrong rotation direction, or limit violation

9. **Lift-Reposition-Reengage Quality** (1-5): Between CW strokes, did the robot lift before performing CCW reposition? Was CCW reposition performed only at the approach height (not at engage height)? Did the robot re-engage at the correct screw XY position after repositioning?
   - 5 = clean lift → CCW → lower sequence every cycle, correct positions throughout
   - 3 = mostly correct but one cycle had minor sequencing or positional issues
   - 1 = CCW at engage height (unscrewing), failed to re-engage, or wrong position after reposition

10. **Cycle Completion** (1-5): Were all {n_cycles} screwing cycles completed? What was the total CW rotation achieved?
    - 5 = all cycles completed, total rotation consistent with expected cumulative angle
    - 3 = most cycles completed or total rotation is lower than expected but non-trivial
    - 1 = fewer than half the cycles completed or negligible total rotation

### D. Trajectory Quality — General

11. **Smoothness** (1-5): Are joint angle transitions gradual and physically realistic throughout the entire trajectory? Are there sudden large jumps that suggest jerky or unsafe motion?
    - 5 = all transitions are smooth and gradual
    - 3 = occasional abrupt changes but generally acceptable
    - 1 = frequent large jumps suggesting unsafe or unrealistic motion

12. **Efficiency** (1-5): Is the trajectory reasonably direct, or are there unnecessary detours, pauses, or redundant motions? Is the velocity scaling appropriate for each phase (slower for engagement, faster for repositioning)?
    - 5 = efficient with appropriate speed variation per phase
    - 3 = some unnecessary motions but overall acceptable timing
    - 1 = highly inefficient with excessive detours or uniform slow speed throughout

### E. Reasoning-Trajectory Alignment

13. **Consistency** (1-5): Does the actual trajectory follow the plan described in the Cosmos reasoning output? If the reasoning identifies specific phases, do they correspond to what the trajectory shows? If corrective waypoints were suggested, are they consistent with what would fix the identified issues?
    - 5 = reasoning and trajectory match closely in phase boundaries, directions, and positions
    - 3 = general alignment but some phases described in reasoning don't match trajectory timing or values
    - 1 = reasoning describes a fundamentally different trajectory than what was executed
