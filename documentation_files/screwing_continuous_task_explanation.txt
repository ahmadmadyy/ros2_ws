Robotic Continuous Screwing Task — Detailed Explanation
Robot: UR5e 6-DOF manipulator with Robotiq 85 parallel gripper
Script: tests/test_screw_continuous.py


--- Overview ---

The continuous screwing task is a more realistic version of the basic
top-down screwing task. The core problem it solves is a physical one:
a robot wrist has a finite joint range, so it cannot just spin forever
in one direction. At some point the joint limit is hit and the motion
has to stop. The continuous script handles this by simulating how a
human would actually drive a long screw — turn as far as the wrist
allows, lift the screwdriver slightly off the screw to disengage,
rewind the wrist back to the starting position while lifted, then
re-engage and drive again. This cycle repeats N_CYCLES times (5 by
default).

The pick sequence (steps 1 through 10) is identical to the basic
top-down task. What changes is everything after the screwdriver is
engaged with the screw. Instead of executing a fixed number of turns
and stopping, the script runs a full cycle loop that handles the wrist
limit problem. After the last cycle, the gripper is opened to drop the
screwdriver, the object is detached from the planning scene, and the
arm returns home.

The screwdriver geometry and target location are the same as the
top-down task: 24 mm diameter shaft at x=0.45, y=0.00, screw target
at x=0.40, y=0.10 flush with the table, grasp at the shaft midpoint
with tool0 at z=0.26.


--- The 14 Steps ---

Steps 1–10 are identical to the top-down task. They are included here
for completeness.


Step 1 — Home position

The arm starts from the canonical home configuration:
[0, -90°, 0°, -90°, 0°, 0°]. Every run begins and ends here. It is a
safe, collision-free posture from which all subsequent IK solutions
are seeded.


Step 2 — Open gripper

The gripper is fully opened before any approach to avoid bumping the
screwdriver during the descent.


Step 3 — Approach screwdriver (top-down)

The arm moves to 18 cm above the grasp point (tool0 at z=0.44) with
a top-down orientation (180° rotation about world X). A PICK_SEED is
used to keep the IK solver in the correct arm configuration branch for
the pick sequence.


Step 4 — Allow gripper/screwdriver collision

The collision between the gripper links and the screwdriver object is
allowed in the MoveIt planning scene. Without this the planner would
reject the grasp as a collision.


Step 5 — Descend to grasp

The arm descends to the grasp pose (tool0 at z=0.26, finger tips at
shaft midpoint z=0.09) at reduced speed (0.2 velocity scaling). The IK
result from the approach step is reused as the seed.


Step 6 — Close gripper onto shaft

The gripper closes to 0.57 rad, approximately a 24 mm gap, matching the
screwdriver diameter.


Step 7 — Attach screwdriver to tool0 in planning scene

The screwdriver object is attached to the tool0 link in MoveIt so it
moves with the robot and is included in all subsequent collision checks.


Step 8 — Retreat upward

The arm lifts 20 cm above the grasp point (tool0 at z=0.46) before
any horizontal translation.


Step 9 — Move above screw target

The arm moves to directly above the screw location with a 15 cm safety
clearance (tool0 at z=0.41), keeping the same top-down orientation.


Step 10 — Lower to engage screw (initial)

The arm descends to the engagement pose (tool0 at z=0.26, tip at z=0.0)
at 0.15 velocity scaling. The joint configuration at this pose is saved
as the reference for all subsequent cycle operations. The wrist_3 value
at this point is also saved as the initial_wrist3 reference, used for
all orientation calculations throughout the cycle loop.


Step 11 — Continuous screw cycles (N_CYCLES = 5)

This is the main difference from the top-down task. Rather than a fixed
number of rotation steps, a cycle loop runs that handles the wrist joint
limit. Each cycle has four sub-steps:

  (a) CW rotation to joint limit

      Starting from the current engage joint configuration, wrist_3 is
      decremented by 45° per step (π/4 rad) until the next step would
      exceed the safe minimum (−5.983 rad, which is 2π minus a 0.3 rad
      safety margin). The number of steps per stroke varies depending on
      where wrist_3 is at the start of each cycle — typically around 15
      steps (about 675°, or roughly 1.9 full turns) per stroke.

      The 45° step size is smaller than the 90° used in the basic task.
      Finer steps give smoother motion and more precise control near the
      joint limit.

  (b) Small lift — 6 cm upward

      Once the CW limit is reached, the screwdriver tip is lifted 6 cm
      off the screw (tool0 moves from z=0.26 to z=0.32). This
      disengages the tip so the wrist can be rewound without dragging
      the screwdriver against the screw.

      The critical part here is how the lift pose is computed. The
      orientation passed to IK is not the original top-down quaternion —
      it is a quaternion that encodes the actual current wrist_3 rotation
      relative to the initial value. Specifically:

          q_rot = (cos(Δ/2), -sin(Δ/2), 0, 0)

      where Δ = current_wrist3 − initial_wrist3. By targeting this
      orientation, the IK solver finds a configuration where wrist_3
      stays at its current rotated value and only the shoulder and elbow
      adjust to raise the z height. Without this trick, the IK would
      try to return the end-effector to the original top-down orientation
      and would move wrist_3 back in the process, which would undo the
      screwing work.

  (c) CCW wrist_3 reset — wrist only

      With the tip lifted clear of the screw, wrist_3 is commanded
      directly to WRIST3_SAFE_MAX (+5.983 rad) in a single joint move.
      Only joint 5 changes — the rest of the arm stays in the lifted
      configuration. This is the rewind step: the wrist travels from
      its fully wound CW position back to its fully wound CCW limit,
      giving it the maximum possible range for the next CW stroke. The
      velocity scaling here is higher (0.5) because the motion is a
      pure joint move with no Cartesian constraint and no risk of
      collision with the screw.

  (d) Lower back to engage height

      After the CCW reset, the arm descends back to the engagement height
      (tool0 at z=0.26) to re-engage the screwdriver tip with the screw.
      The orientation passed to IK is again computed from q_from_wrist3_delta
      using WRIST3_SAFE_MAX as the current wrist_3 value, which tells the
      solver to find a configuration where wrist_3 is near +5.983 rad.
      The result becomes the new engage joint configuration for the next
      cycle's CW stroke.

      On the last cycle (cycle 5), step (d) is skipped. Instead of
      lowering back to engage height, the arm lifts to the full approach
      height (tool0 at z=0.41) to prepare for a safe home return. There
      is no point re-engaging the screw if no more cycles are coming.


Step 12 — Open gripper — drop screwdriver

After all cycles are complete, the gripper is opened to release the
screwdriver. In the basic top-down task the screwdriver stays held
until the arm returns home. Here it is explicitly dropped because the
intent is to leave the screw driven and release the tool.


Step 13 — Detach screwdriver from tool0

The screwdriver is detached from the tool0 link in the MoveIt planning
scene and returned to the world as a free object. This must happen after
the gripper opens — detaching first and then opening would leave the
planner in an inconsistent state where the object is attached but the
gripper is open.


Step 14 — Return home

The arm returns to the home joint configuration. At this point the
screwdriver is no longer attached, so the planner treats it as a world
obstacle rather than part of the robot body during this final move.


--- Why the continuous approach is more realistic ---

A real person driving a long screw with a hand screwdriver does exactly
what this script does: turn as far as the wrist allows, lift the
driver, rewind, drop back in, repeat. The basic top-down task avoids
this problem entirely by only doing 3 turns — well within the joint
range — so it never has to deal with the limit. The continuous task is
designed to handle arbitrarily deep screws where the total required
rotation exceeds what any single stroke can deliver.

Each stroke delivers roughly 675° of CW rotation. Five cycles gives
approximately 3375° total (about 9.4 full turns), far more than the 3
turns in the basic task.


--- Key Geometry and Parameters ---

Screwdriver position:    x=0.45, y=0.00 (centre of base)
Screwdriver diameter:    24 mm
Grasp height (tool0):    z=0.26 (finger tips at shaft midpoint z=0.09)
Approach clearance:      18 cm above grasp → tool0 at z=0.44
Retreat clearance:       20 cm above grasp → tool0 at z=0.46

Screw position:          x=0.40, y=0.10, z=0.0 (flush with table)
Engage height (tool0):   z=0.26 (tip at z=0.0)
Pre-screw clearance:     15 cm above engage → tool0 at z=0.41
Lift height per stroke:  6 cm → tool0 at z=0.32 during rewind

Gripper close position:  0.57 rad (~24 mm gap)
Number of cycles:        5
CW step size:            45° (π/4 rad) per step
Wrist_3 safe range:      −5.983 to +5.983 rad (±2π minus 0.3 rad margin)
CCW reset speed:         0.5 velocity scaling (fastest move in sequence)
Engage/disengage speed:  0.15 velocity scaling (slowest move in sequence)
